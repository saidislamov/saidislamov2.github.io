Kyrgyz Manti

Kyrgyz manti has a rich background that is likely rooted in Central Asian nomadic cultures, with possible origins among the Turkic-speaking Uighurs in northwestern China, and its spread along the Silk Road. Nomadic people;, including the ancient Turkic and Mongol horsemen, carried manti with them as a convenient, quick meal that could be steamed or boiled over a campfire. Today, it is a beloved traditional dish in Kyrgyzstan, often featuring a filling of ground meat (typically lamb or beef), onions, and sometimes potatoes or pumpkin.

For the filling

  • 1 lb of ground beef
  • 2-3 onions, finely chopped
  • 1 medium potato,finely diced (optional but traditional)
  • 1 teaspoon of salt(adjust to taste)
  • 1 teaspoon of black pepper
  • 2-3 tablespoons of melted butter

Preparation

  • Divide the dough into several smaller balls.
  • Roll them into thin sheets.
  • Place a spoonful of filling in the center.
  • Fold them up, to form a traditional manti shape.

Steaming

  • Apply butter to the sides of the steamer, so it does not stick.
  • Place them with a little space in between.
  • Steam for 40-45 minutes until fully cooked.
